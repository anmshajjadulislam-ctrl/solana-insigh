What is Solana?

Solana is a public, permissionless Layer 1 blockchain designed to support decentralized applications, digital assets and fast transaction processing. SOL is used for transaction fees, staking and participation in the network.

Consensus Mechanism

How Solana validators coordinate and agree on network state.

01

CURRENT CONSENSUS

Alpenglow

Alpenglow is Solana's newer consensus architecture. Its Votor component handles validator voting and transaction finality through stake-weighted certificates.

Official explanation ↗

PROOF OF STAKE

PoS

Validators stake SOL to participate in network security. Stake weight influences validator voting power and helps protect the network.

HISTORICAL TECHNOLOGY

Proof of History

PoH provides a cryptographically verifiable sequence of events and served as a timing mechanism in Solana's earlier Tower BFT architecture.

02 / TOKEN ECONOMICS

Supply & Distribution

Understanding SOL issuance, utility and inflation.

02
GENESIS SUPPLY

500 Million SOL

The genesis supply established the initial SOL token base when the network launched.

SUPPLY MODEL

Inflationary

New SOL can be issued through the protocol's inflation schedule to support staking rewards.

MAXIMUM SUPPLY

No Fixed Cap

Solana does not have a fixed maximum supply. Supply changes through issuance and token burns.

Solana Inflation Schedule

The published inflation schedule starts at 8% annually, decreases by 15% year-over-year and approaches a long-term inflation rate of 1.5%.

Initial inflation rate 8%
Annual disinflation rate -15%
Long-term inflation rate 1.5%

These are protocol schedule parameters, not a live measurement of the current effective inflation rate.

04 / SMART CONTRACT SECURITY

Common Vulnerabilities

Security risks developers should understand when building Solana programs.

04
HIGH RISK

Missing Signer Checks

A program may allow an unauthorized user to perform an action if it fails to verify the required signer.

Mitigation

Verify signer identity and authorization before sensitive operations.

HIGH RISK

Account Validation Errors

Incorrect account ownership or relationship checks may allow attackers to supply unexpected accounts.

Mitigation

Validate account ownership, addresses, relationships and expected data.

MEDIUM RISK

Input Validation Issues

Malicious or unexpected input values can cause incorrect calculations or unauthorized program behavior.

Mitigation

Check data types, ranges, arithmetic operations and business rules.

MEDIUM RISK

Private Key Exposure

Exposed private keys can allow attackers to control accounts and transfer assets without permission.

Mitigation

Use secure key management, hardware wallets and encrypted backups.

Security principle: Never trust accounts or input supplied by a user without verification. Follow Solana's official security guidance and test programs before deployment.
05 / DIGITAL ASSET SAFETY

Wallet Risk Comparison

Comparing custodial exchange wallets and self-custody wallets.

05
▣
THIRD-PARTY CUSTODY

Custodial Wallet

A service provider or cryptocurrency exchange controls the private keys on behalf of the user.

Advantages

Convenient access, account recovery and customer support.

Risks

Exchange hacks, insolvency, withdrawal restrictions and account freezes.

Private key control Service Provider
◇
USER CONTROLLED

Self-Custody Wallet

The user controls the private keys or recovery phrase and authorizes transactions directly.

Advantages

Direct asset control, independence and reduced exchange custody risk.

Risks

Lost recovery phrase, phishing, malware and unauthorized signing.

Private key control User
Conclusion: Custodial wallets offer convenience but introduce third-party custody risk. Self-custody offers greater direct control, but the user becomes responsible for securing private keys.
